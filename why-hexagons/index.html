What is allowed to be an input

Every film carries the same tension. A vertex creeps in the direction of the unbalanced pull. Three equal pulls cancel only when the films stand at 120° to each other. Nothing in the step writes down that number. Changing the tension changes how fast the creep is. It does not move the angle the creep is heading for.

The opening frame is a Voronoi foam of scattered seeds, or a square grid. A hexagonal lattice is not a seed. A pentagon stuck to a heptagon is allowed to remain: it is the scar a neighbour-switch leaves, and it still has to meet at 120°.

Why six, and why that is not the discovery

On a torus with three films at every vertex, the average cell has exactly six sides. That is Euler's formula, true of the crooked first frame. The page prints it so it is not mistaken for a result of the relaxation. The result is the angle. A hexagon is what you get when that angle and six sides land on the same cell, and the pressures match so the sides are straight.

The second act

Turn on breathe and the target areas change by von Neumann's rule: cells with more than six sides grow, cells with fewer shrink, six-sided cells hold. The rate is a clock you set. A physical permeability for these films was not sourced, and the page does not invent one.

Simplifications

Two dimensions. Real foams are three-dimensional, and four films meet along an edge at the tetrahedral angle; that angle is not a mode here. Films have no thickness. The same tension on every film, no gravity, no temperature, no wax. Vertices have no mass. The sample is a torus, so a wall cannot pin a corner at 90°. Coarsening is only the (n − 6) rule. The length that triggers a neighbour switch is a numerical cutoff, not a measured length.

This is not a bee, and it is not a proof of the honeycomb conjecture. Whether wax flows into hexagons on its own is a dispute in the literature; the sources are in the fact sheet, and they do not agree. The standfirst leaves them out.
